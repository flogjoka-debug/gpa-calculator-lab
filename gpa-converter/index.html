How to use the GPA converter

  1. Enter your GPA as your school reports it.
  2. Pick the scale it's on, such as 5.0 or 10, or choose Other and enter the top of your scale.
  3. Pick the scale you want, 4.0 unless you need another, and convert. You'll get the converted GPA and the math behind it.

How to convert a GPA to a 4.0 scale

Divide your GPA by the top of your scale, then multiply by 4. Your place on the scale stays the same: a student 90% of the way up a 5.0 scale is also 90% of the way up a 4.0 scale.

Converting from 4.0 to another scale

The same formula works in reverse: multiply by the top of the new scale and divide by 4. A 3.2 on a 4.0 scale is 3.2 × 10 ÷ 4 = 8.0 on a 10-point scale. Pick the scale you need under "Convert to".

When a straight conversion gives the wrong answer

  • A weighted high school GPA. A 4.5 on a weighted 5.0 scale includes bonus points for Honors and AP classes, and dividing it down doesn't remove them. Recalculate from your grades in the high school GPA calculator, which shows the unweighted GPA next to the weighted one.
  • A percentage average. An 85% average isn't a 3.4. On the common U.S. scale an 85 is a B, worth 3.0. Use the percentage to letter grade calculator to find the letter first.
  • A scale where lower is better. In Germany, for example, 1.0 is the best grade and 4.0 is the lowest pass, so multiplying would turn a top student into a weak one. Use the method your target school or credential evaluator names.
  • A scale where the passing mark sits high up. The formula assumes every point on the scale is worth the same, which isn't true everywhere.

How colleges handle GPAs from other scales

U.S. colleges don't rely on a number you converted yourself. Many recalculate a GPA from the grades on your transcript with their own rules, and applicants with transcripts from outside the U.S. are often asked for a course-by-course evaluation from a credential evaluation service. The Common App asks for your GPA on your school's own scale, with options from 4 up to 20 and 100, so there's no need to convert it there. Our guide to weighted vs. unweighted GPA covers which number to report.

When the converter can mislead you

A 4.5 on a weighted 5.0 high school scale converts to 3.60, but that 4.5 already includes bonus points for Honors and AP classes. The student's unweighted GPA might be 3.8 or 3.4, depending on which classes earned the bonus. For a weighted GPA, recalculate from your grades in the high school GPA calculator instead of converting the total.

Converting a GPA from a 4.33 scale

A 4.33 scale only differs from a 4.0 scale at the top: an A+ is worth 4.33 instead of 4.0, and every other grade keeps its usual value. That makes a proportional conversion misleading. A student with straight A's has a 4.00 on both scales, but 4.0 × 4 ÷ 4.33 = 3.70. For a 4.33 scale, count each A+ as 4.0 and recalculate instead, or simply use the GPA your school reports, which is usually close.

Converting a percentage average to a 4.0 GPA

Dividing a percentage by 25 is a common shortcut, but it isn't how U.S. schools calculate GPA. They turn each class grade into a letter and each letter into grade points. Take three classes with final grades of 92, 85 and 78:

Class gradeLetter on the common scaleGrade points

85%B3.0

GPA(3.7 + 3.0 + 2.3) ÷ 3 = 3.00

The percentage average is 85%, and 85 ÷ 25 = 3.40, well above the 3.00 the letters give. Use the percentage to letter grade calculator for each class first, then the GPA calculator for the average.

Transcripts from outside the U.S.

Universities in many countries report grades on scales that don't map neatly onto 4.0: a 10-point CGPA, a 20-point scale, percentages, or a scale where the lowest number is the best grade. U.S. colleges and graduate schools often ask applicants with these transcripts for an evaluation from a credential evaluation service, such as World Education Services (WES) or Educational Credential Evaluators (ECE), and use that result instead of a GPA you converted yourself. Check what each school asks for before you pay for an evaluation, because some programs name the service they accept.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a 5.0 GPA to a 4.0 scale?

Multiply by 4 and divide by 5. A 4.5 out of 5.0 becomes 3.60. If your 5.0 scale is a weighted high school scale, recalculate from your grades instead, because the bonus points stay in the number.

What is a 3.5 out of 5 on a 4.0 scale?

3.5 × 4 ÷ 5 = 2.80.

How do I convert a 10-point CGPA to a 4.0 GPA?

Multiply by 4 and divide by 10, so an 8.0 becomes 3.20. Treat it as an estimate: universities that admit students from 10-point systems usually ask for the transcript or an evaluation and convert it themselves.

Is a converted GPA official?

No. It's a proportional estimate. The official number is the one your school, or the receiving school's evaluator, calculates.

How do I convert a GPA from a 4.5 scale?

Multiply by 4 and divide by 4.5. A 4.1 on a 4.5 scale is 3.64 on a 4.0 scale. If the 4.5 scale is a weighted high school scale, recalculate from your grades instead.

Can I convert a percentage average to a 4.0 GPA?

Not with this formula. Find the letter first with the percentage to letter grade calculator, then use that letter's grade points.

What is a 9.0 CGPA on a 4.0 scale?

A proportional conversion gives 9.0 × 4 ÷ 10 = 3.60. Treat it as an estimate; the school you apply to may use a credential evaluation instead.

How do I convert a GPA on a 4.33 scale?

Don't scale it down. Only an A+ is worth more than 4.0 on that scale, so count each A+ as 4.0 and recalculate, or use the GPA your school reports.
