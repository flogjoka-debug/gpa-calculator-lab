When to use a grade calculator

Use it whenever you want to know where you stand before the official gradebook updates: after a test comes back, before a big assignment is due, or when you're deciding where to spend your study time. It works for any class that grades by points or by weighted categories, in high school or college.

How to use the grade calculator

  1. Pick Points if your teacher adds up points earned and possible, or Weighted categories if your syllabus gives each category a percentage, such as tests 50% and homework 20%.
  2. Enter your scores. With categories, enter each category's average and its weight. Leave a category blank if nothing in it is graded yet.
  3. Click Calculate grade. You'll see your current grade, the math behind it and how much of the course it covers so far.

How we calculated this

In Points mode we add up the points you earned and divide by the points possible. In Weighted categories mode each category average is multiplied by its weight, and the results are added together. If some categories don't have a score yet, we divide by the weight that's been graded so far.

How to calculate your grade in a class

If your teacher grades by total points:

  1. Add up the points you earned.
  2. Add up the points possible for the same assignments.
  3. Divide earned by possible and multiply by 100.

If your syllabus uses weighted categories:

  1. Find each category's average, such as tests, homework and quizzes.
  2. Multiply each average by the category's weight written as a decimal (30% is 0.30).
  3. Add the results. If some categories have no grades yet, divide by the total weight you've used.

Example: points and weighted categories

Points: 18 out of 20 and 30 out of 40 add up to 48 out of 60, which is 80%.

Categories: an 80% average in a category worth 40% and a 90% average in a category worth 60% give 0.40 × 80 + 0.60 × 90 = 86%.

Weighted grades explained

A weighted category counts for a fixed share of your grade no matter how many assignments it holds. Say your syllabus gives tests 50%, homework 30% and quizzes 20%, and your averages are 82, 95 and 88.

0.50 × 82 = 41. 0.30 × 95 = 28.5. 0.20 × 88 = 17.6.

41 + 28.5 + 17.6 = 87.1%, a B+ on the common scale. Your high homework average helps, but the tests still decide most of the grade.

A blank isn't a zero

Leave a score blank if the assignment hasn't been graded yet. We'll leave it out and tell you how much of the course your grade is based on. Enter 0 only if you really got a zero, because it pulls your grade down like any other score.

When your weights don't add up to 100%

If the categories you entered add up to less than 100%, the result is your grade so far. If they add up to more than 100%, check your syllabus. One of the weights is probably wrong.

Example: a syllabus with three weighted categories

A common syllabus split is homework 20%, quizzes 30% and tests 50%. Here's how a student with averages of 95, 84 and 78 ends up:

Example: a syllabus with three weighted categories
CategoryWeightYour averagePoints toward your grade
Homework20%95%19.0
Quizzes30%84%25.2
Tests50%78%39.0
Total100%—83.2%

That's a B on the common scale. Raising the homework average to 100 would only add 1 point; raising the test average to 88 would add 5.

Grade calculator for college classes

College syllabi often mix both systems: a points total inside each category, then category weights on top. Work out each category's average from its points first, then use Weighted categories mode. Watch for three things college courses often do:

  • They drop the lowest quiz or homework score. Leave that score out before you average the category.
  • They count participation or attendance as its own category. Give it the weight the syllabus lists.
  • They set a minimum score on the final to pass the course. That rule isn't part of the average, so check it separately.

What-if: the score you need on what's left

If a category isn't graded yet, you can test scores for it. Say homework (20%) and quizzes (30%) are done at 95 and 84, and the test category (50%) is still open. You have 19 + 25.2 = 44.2 points so far. To finish with a 90, the tests have to bring 45.8 points, which means a test average of 91.6. For one final exam, the final grade calculator does this for you.

How one test score changes your grade

In a points-based class, a new test changes your grade by how far its score sits from your current average, scaled by its size. Say you have 340 of 400 points so far (85%) and the next test is worth 100 points:

How one test score changes your grade
Test scoreNew totalNew grade
95 out of 100435 of 50087.0%
85 out of 100425 of 50085.0%
70 out of 100410 of 50082.0%

A test that matches your average leaves the grade where it is. The bigger the test compared with the points already graded, the further it moves you.

Why your gradebook may disagree

  • Your teacher drops the lowest quiz or homework score before averaging.
  • Extra credit adds points earned without adding points possible.
  • Late penalties or missing work count as zeros in the gradebook but not here.
  • A category your teacher hasn't graded yet is left out here, while some gradebooks count it as zero.
  • Your school rounds the final percentage, for example 89.5 to 90, before giving a letter.

If the numbers still don't match, ask your teacher how the gradebook is set up. It's a quick question, and it tells you exactly which scores to work on.

How to raise your grade

  • Turn in missing work first. A zero hurts more than a low score: in a homework category of ten equal assignments, one missing assignment pulls the category average down by up to 10 points.
  • Put your time where the weight is. A 5-point gain on tests worth 50% adds 2.5 points to your grade; the same gain on homework worth 20% adds 1.
  • Ask about retakes, corrections or extra credit before the last weeks of the term.
  • Plan your final with the final grade calculator, which shows the score you need for each letter.

Dropped scores and how they change your grade

Many teachers drop the lowest quiz or homework score. Quiz scores of 70, 85, 90 and 95 average 85, but with the 70 dropped the average is (85 + 90 + 95) ÷ 3 = 90. If your syllabus drops a score, leave the lowest one out here so your result matches the gradebook.

What to look for in your syllabus

  • The categories and their weights, or a note that the class uses total points.
  • Whether any scores are dropped, and how many.
  • How extra credit, late work and missing work are counted.
  • The cutoffs for each letter, and whether the final percentage is rounded.

With those four things, the calculator gives the same grade your teacher will. To combine two quarter grades and an exam into a semester grade, use the semester grade calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much will one assignment change my grade?

In a points-based class, it depends on how many points are already in the gradebook. With 48 out of 60 points (80%), scoring 10 out of 20 on a quiz brings you to 58 out of 80, which is 72.5%. Later in the term, the same quiz would move your grade less.

What's the difference between points-based and weighted grading?

With points, a 100-point test counts five times as much as a 20-point quiz. With weighted categories, each category gets its share of the grade, so ten small quizzes can count as much as one big test.

Can I use this for a college class?

Yes. Use the weights from your course syllabus. The math is the same in high school and college.

What should I do next with my grade?

If a final exam is coming up, use this grade to plan your final. To see the letter for a percentage on your teacher's scale, convert a percentage to a letter grade.

What grade is 12 out of 15?

12 ÷ 15 = 0.80, or 80%. On the common scale that's a B−, and on a 10-point scale it's a B. Use the percentage to letter grade calculator to check your school's cutoffs.

How do I calculate my grade if I don't know the weights?

Check the syllabus or your school's online gradebook first; most list the categories and their weights. If the class simply adds up points, use the Points tab. Guessing equal weights can be far off when tests count much more than homework.

Does extra credit count?

Extra credit usually adds to the points you earned without adding to the points possible. In the Points tab, add it to a score's earned points and leave its possible points as they were.

Is a D a passing grade?

At many schools a D passes the class and earns credit. Some majors and prerequisites require a C or better, so check your program's rules before you count on it.
