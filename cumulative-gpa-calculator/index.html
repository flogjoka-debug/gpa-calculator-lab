How to use the cumulative GPA calculator

  1. Enter what you have so far: your quality points and GPA credits from your transcript for an exact result, or your current GPA and credits for an estimate.
  2. Add each new semester, class by class or as a semester GPA with its credits.
  3. Click Calculate. You'll see your new cumulative GPA, how much it changed and the math behind it.

What is a cumulative GPA?

Your cumulative GPA is the average of every graded class you've taken at a school, weighted by credits. A semester GPA covers one term; the cumulative GPA covers all of them together.

How we calculated this

A cumulative GPA is total quality points divided by total GPA credits. We add the quality points you had to the new ones, add the credits the same way, and divide. Averaging two GPAs only works when both cover the same number of credits.

How to calculate cumulative GPA

  1. Find the GPA credits and quality points you have so far on your transcript. If it only shows your GPA, multiply that GPA by your GPA credits to estimate the quality points.
  2. Work out the quality points for your new semester: grade points × credits for each class, added up.
  3. Add the old and new quality points together.
  4. Add the old and new GPA credits together.
  5. Divide total quality points by total GPA credits.

Example: 18 credits at 3.0, then 6 credits at 4.0

So far: 3.0 × 18 = 54 quality points. New semester: 4.0 × 6 = 24.

Together: 78 ÷ 24 = 3.25.

Averaging 3.0 and 4.0 would give 3.5. That's too high, because the first 18 credits weigh three times as much as the new 6.

How much can one semester raise your GPA?

It depends on how many credits you already have. Say you have a 3.0 over 60 credits and earn straight A's in 15 credits. That's 3.0 × 60 = 180 quality points plus 4.0 × 15 = 60, so (180 + 60) ÷ 75 = 3.20. The same semester after only 15 earlier credits would move you much further. The more credits behind you, the slower your GPA changes.

Where to find GPA credits on your transcript

Look for "GPA hours", "GPA credits" or "quality hours". That's the number of credits that carry a letter grade. It can be lower than "earned hours", which may also include pass/fail and transfer credit. Use the GPA number.

If your transcript lists quality points, enter them. You'll get an exact result instead of an estimate.

Don't count a semester twice

If a semester is already part of the GPA and credits you entered, leave it out of the new semesters.

Transfer credits and your cumulative GPA

Many colleges list transfer credits on your transcript without counting their grades in your GPA there. If that's how your school works, leave transfer classes out of this calculator, or your result will be higher or lower than the one your registrar shows. Law school is different: LSAC combines grades from every college you attended, so use the law school GPA calculator for that number.

Quarter and semester units

The math works the same on the quarter system: quality points ÷ GPA units. Just don't mix the two. If some of your credits are quarter units and some are semester units, convert first. LSAC, for example, multiplies quarter hours by 0.67 to get semester hours.

Cumulative GPA from semester GPAs

Don't average your semester GPAs. Weight each one by its credits instead, because a 15-credit semester counts for more than a 12-credit one.

The plain average of 3.20 and 3.80 is 3.50, but the cumulative GPA is 93.6 ÷ 27 = 3.47. The calculator does this for you when you enter each semester's GPA and credits.

Is cumulative GPA weighted or unweighted?

In college it's almost always unweighted, because colleges don't add bonus points for harder classes. In high school it depends on the school: some report a weighted cumulative GPA, some an unweighted one, and many show both. Whatever you enter here, keep it consistent. Combining a weighted GPA so far with unweighted new grades gives a number that matches neither.

Mistakes that throw off a cumulative GPA

  • Using earned or attempted hours instead of GPA hours. Pass, withdrawal and transfer credits often count toward your degree but not your GPA.
  • Counting both attempts of a class your college replaced. Enter only the attempt that counts.
  • Entering a semester twice: once inside the GPA so far and again as a new semester.
  • Rounding your GPA so far. A 3.4 over 60 credits and a 3.449 over 60 credits can give different results, so use the quality points if your transcript shows them.

CGPA and cumulative GPA

CGPA is another name for cumulative GPA. In some countries it's reported on a 10-point scale instead of 4.0. If you need to compare a 10-point CGPA with a U.S. GPA, the GPA converter gives a proportional estimate, and the receiving school may calculate its own.

How to plan a target cumulative GPA

Work backward from the GPA you want: (target × total credits after next term − quality points so far) ÷ credits next term. With a 2.8 over 45 credits and a 3.0 target, a 15-credit term needs (3.0 × 60 − 126) ÷ 15 = 3.6. If the answer is above 4.0, one term can't get you there. The target GPA planner on our main calculator does the math and shows how many terms of A's it would take.

Cumulative GPA in high school

High schools keep a cumulative GPA too, across every year you've finished. Each semester class is often worth half a credit, so a year of six classes adds about 6 credits. If your school weights Honors and AP classes, use the high school GPA calculator for each new semester, then bring the result here with its credits. Use the same kind of GPA, weighted or unweighted, for both parts.

Frequently asked questions

Is cumulative GPA the same as overall GPA?

Usually, yes. Both mean the GPA across all graded terms at one school. Some transcripts also show a separate GPA for your major.

Why isn't my cumulative GPA the average of my semester GPAs?

Because semesters carry different numbers of credits. A 12-credit semester and an 18-credit semester don't count equally, so averaging the two GPAs gives the wrong answer.

Does my cumulative GPA include transfer credits?

At many colleges it doesn't. Transfer credits count toward your degree, but only grades earned at your current school go into its GPA. Check your registrar's policy.

Do high schools have a cumulative GPA too?

Yes. The math is the same: total quality points across all your semesters divided by total credits.

Is CGPA the same as cumulative GPA?

Yes. CGPA is short for cumulative grade point average. Some countries report it on a 10-point scale, which the GPA converter can estimate on a 4.0 scale.

How do I find my cumulative GPA?

Your unofficial transcript or student portal shows it, usually with your GPA hours and quality points. Those two numbers give the exact starting point for this calculator.

What is a good cumulative GPA?

It depends on the goal: keeping financial aid, getting into a major or applying to graduate school each come with their own minimum. Our guide to what counts as a good GPA has examples.
