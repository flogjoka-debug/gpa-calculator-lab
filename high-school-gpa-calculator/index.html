What is a high school GPA?

Your high school GPA is the average of your grade points across the classes your school counts, usually on a 4.0 scale. Many schools report two versions: an unweighted GPA, where an A is 4.0 in every class, and a weighted GPA, where Honors, AP, IB or dual enrollment classes earn extra points. Your report card or transcript shows which one your school uses.

How to use the high school GPA calculator

  1. Enter each class's grade and pick its level: Regular, Honors, AP, IB or Dual enrollment.
  2. Set the credits. Leave every class at 1 if your school counts them equally, or use your school's credit values.
  3. Press Calculate GPA. Your unweighted and weighted GPA appear side by side, with the bonus each class added.

Your school's bonus values may differ from the examples. Open "Bonus points (example)" to set them.

A weighted GPA calculator that also shows your unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA treats every class the same: an A is 4.0 in a regular class and in AP. A weighted GPA adds bonus points for harder classes, so it can go above 4.0. This calculator shows both numbers for the same classes. For a full explanation with worked examples, read weighted vs unweighted GPA.

How we calculated this

For the unweighted GPA we use each grade's base points. For the weighted GPA we add the bonus to each Honors, AP, IB or dual enrollment class first. Then both are weighted by credits and divided by total credits.

How to calculate a weighted GPA

A weighted GPA adds bonus points for harder classes before averaging.

  1. Convert each grade to base points: A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0.
  2. Add your school's bonus to each Honors, AP, IB or dual enrollment class. A common setup is +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP and IB.
  3. Multiply each class's points by its credits.
  4. Add the results and divide by your total credits.

For the unweighted GPA, skip step 2.

Example: a regular A and an AP B

Unweighted: (4.0 + 3.0) ÷ 2 = 3.50.

Weighted, with 1.0 added for AP: the B counts as 4.0, so (4.0 + 4.0) ÷ 2 = 4.00.

How much is an AP class worth on a GPA?

At schools that weight AP classes, an AP class usually adds 1.0 point, so an A in AP counts as 5.0 instead of 4.0. Honors classes often add 0.5. Some schools add less, cap the number of weighted classes, or don't weight at all. Set the bonus fields above to match your school and the weighted GPA updates.

Which number do colleges use?

Many colleges recalculate your GPA with their own rules, so the number on your transcript may not be the one an admissions office works from. Report the GPA your school prints, and use both numbers here to understand where you stand.

Details that change the result

Some schools give no bonus for a D or F in a weighted class. Turn on "No bonus for a D or F" if yours does.

If your classes have different credit values, enter them. A full-year class often carries twice the credit of a semester class.

Plus and minus grades use the example scale unless you edit it.

Where the bonus values come from

There's no national rule for weighting. Each school or program sets its own, and two common ones disagree:

  • The University of California adds one honors point for each semester of an approved honors course with an A, B or C, and caps the total at 8. See the UC GPA calculator.
  • Florida's Bright Futures scholarship recalculates a weighted GPA in 16 college-prep credits, adding 0.5 for Honors, AP, IB, AICE and dual enrollment courses, not a full point. Source: Broward County Public Schools: Bright Futures requirements.

So the same transcript can produce three different weighted GPAs: your school's, UC's and a scholarship's. Set the bonus fields above to your school's values to see the number on your report card, and use the program's own rules when you're checking an application.

What is a good high school GPA?

The average U.S. high school graduate finished with a 3.11 in 2019, according to the federal High School Transcript Study. That figure doesn't say how each school weighted its grades, so compare your unweighted GPA with it. What counts as good beyond that depends on the colleges and scholarships you're aiming for; our guide to what counts as a good GPA puts the common numbers in context.

How to raise your high school GPA

  • Bring up the grades in your lowest classes first. Moving a C to a B adds as much to the GPA as moving a B to an A.
  • Take Honors or AP classes where you can keep the grade. The bonus only helps your weighted GPA, and a lower grade can cost more than the bonus adds.
  • Ask about retakes. Some schools replace a failed grade when you retake the class, others average both.
  • Check every grade on your transcript. A missing or wrong grade is easier to fix early.

The target GPA planner shows what you'd need next semester to reach a number.

Do freshman grades count?

Most high schools include every year, 9th grade too, in the GPA on your transcript. Colleges may not use that number as is. Some recalculate your GPA with their own rules, and the University of California, for example, counts only A-G courses taken in 10th and 11th grade. You can estimate your UC GPA separately.

Semester grades or final grades?

Enter the grades your transcript shows. If it lists a grade for each semester, enter each semester as its own row, usually worth half a credit. If it lists one grade for the whole year, enter that grade once with the year's credit. Mixing the two counts some classes twice. Not sure what a semester grade will be yet? Work it out first with the semester grade calculator.

Why your result might not match your report card

  • Your school's bonus values differ from the examples, or it gives no bonus for a D or F.
  • Your school leaves some classes out, such as PE, pass/fail courses or classes taken in middle school.
  • Your school rounds each semester grade before converting it to points.
  • Your school uses 4.33 for an A+ or different plus and minus values. Edit the scale above to match.

Frequently asked questions

Is a weighted GPA out of 5.0?

Often, when AP and IB classes add a full point. But the maximum depends on your school's bonuses, so a weighted GPA might top out at 4.5, 5.0 or something else.

Do all four years of high school count?

For your school's cumulative GPA, usually yes. Some colleges recalculate with only certain years. UC, for example, uses grades from the summer after 9th grade through the summer after 11th grade.

How do I calculate my high school GPA without credits?

Leave every class at 1 credit. Each class then counts the same, which is how many report cards without credits work.

Does a weighted GPA look better to colleges?

Colleges see the classes you took, not just the number. Many recalculate GPAs with their own rules, so a higher weighted GPA only helps when it reflects harder classes that the college also credits.

Do AP exam scores count toward my GPA?

No. Your GPA uses the grade you earned in the class. AP exam scores are reported separately and don't change your transcript grade.

Do electives and PE count in my GPA?

At many schools they do, but some leave out PE, pass/fail or non-academic classes. Your student handbook lists which classes your school counts.

Does dual enrollment get extra GPA points?

At some schools. Many weight a dual enrollment class like an AP class, others give no bonus. Set the "Dual enrollment" bonus above to match your school.

Can my GPA be higher than 4.0?

Your weighted GPA can, because Honors, AP and IB classes earn bonus points. Your unweighted GPA tops out at 4.0.
