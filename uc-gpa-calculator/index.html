How to use the UC GPA calculator

  1. Pick your residency, California resident or nonresident, because it decides which classes can earn honors points.
  2. Add one row per semester for each A-G course from the summer after 9th grade through the summer after 11th grade. Choose the semester grade, when you took it and its honors type.
  3. Calculate. You'll see your unweighted UC GPA, your capped GPA and which honors points counted or were cut by the limits.

How UC calculates your GPA

UC uses only A-G courses taken from the summer after 9th grade through the summer after 11th grade. Each semester grade is worth A = 4, B = 3, C = 2 and D = 1, and pluses and minuses are dropped. Then UC adds one extra point for each semester of an approved honors course with an A, B or C, up to 8 points in total and no more than 4 from 10th grade. UC doesn't round the result, so we don't either.

How to calculate your UC GPA step by step

  1. List every A-G course you took from the summer after 9th grade through the summer after 11th grade, one row per semester.
  2. Give each semester grade its points: A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1. Drop pluses and minuses.
  3. Add one honors point for each semester of a UC-approved honors course with an A, B or C. Stop at 8 points in total, and count no more than 4 from 10th grade.
  4. Add the grade points and honors points, then divide by the number of semester grades. Don't round the result.

Example: 12 A's, all honors

A California resident has six honors A's in 10th grade and six in 11th. The 12 grades add up to 48 points, so the unweighted GPA is 48 ÷ 12 = 4.00.

All 12 classes could earn an honors point, but only 8 count. Four come from 10th grade, the most UC allows, and four from 11th. The capped GPA is 56 ÷ 12 = 4.66.

If the same student had only the six 10th-grade A's, just 4 honors points would count.

Which classes earn honors points

For California residents: AP, IB Higher Level, designated IB Standard Level, UC-transferable college courses, and honors courses certified by UC on your school's course list.

For nonresidents: only AP and IB courses, with an A, B or C. School-designated honors classes don't earn UC honors points, though they can still be considered in the rest of your application.

Your school weights classes its own way. You can compare your school's weighted GPA with this one.

Minimum GPA for the University of California

California residents need at least a 3.0 GPA in A-G courses, and nonresidents need at least a 3.4. UC calculates the GPA you're measured against from the grades you report on your application. Source: UC Admissions: GPA requirement.

Why this isn't your official UC GPA

UC calculates the official number from the grades you report in your application. Use this result to check your math and to see which honors points are being cut by the limits.

Example: a full 10th and 11th grade transcript

A California resident has 20 A-G semester grades: 14 A's and 6 B's. Ten of those semesters are in honors courses UC accepts, five in each year, all with an A or B.

Two of the ten honors points are cut: one by the 4-point limit on 10th grade and one by the 8-point total. That's why the calculator lists which honors points counted: it shows when another honors class wouldn't change the number.

Why your school GPA and your UC GPA differ

  • UC leaves out 9th and 12th grade, while your school's GPA usually covers every year.
  • UC drops pluses and minuses, so a B+ and a B− both count as 3 points.
  • UC counts only A-G courses. Electives, PE and other classes outside A-G don't affect it.
  • Your school may give honors points for classes UC doesn't accept, or give more than one point, and it doesn't cap them at 8.

The same transcript can produce a school weighted GPA well above or below the UC capped GPA. Compare them with the high school GPA calculator.

Report your grades exactly as they appear

UC calculates its GPA from the grades you enter on your application, so a grade typed wrong changes the number. Enter each semester grade as your transcript shows it, including any D or F, and follow the application's instructions for anything unusual on your transcript. This calculator uses whatever grades you give it.

Frequently asked questions

Does UC count 9th grade?

No. UC's GPA starts with the summer after 9th grade, and a class taken that summer counts as 10th grade. Classes taken during 9th grade still count for meeting A-G subject requirements, but not toward the GPA.

Does UC count plus and minus grades?

No. A B+ and a B− both count as a B, worth 3 points.

What's the difference between a capped and an uncapped UC GPA?

The capped GPA limits honors points to 8, with no more than 4 from 10th grade. Some counselors and websites also mention an uncapped or fully weighted GPA that counts every honors point. UC's GPA requirement page describes the capped calculation, so that's what this calculator shows.

Do summer classes count toward the UC GPA?

Yes, if they're A-G courses. A class in the summer after 9th or 10th grade counts as 10th grade, and a class in the summer after 11th grade counts as 11th grade.

Does a D count in the UC GPA?

Yes. A D is worth 1 point, but a D in an honors course doesn't earn an honors point.

Does UC count 12th grade?

Not in the GPA. UC's GPA covers A-G courses from the summer after 9th grade through the summer after 11th grade. Your senior-year courses still matter in the rest of your application.

How many honors points can count in the UC GPA?

Up to 8 in total, with no more than 4 from 10th grade. Each semester of an honors course UC accepts, with an A, B or C, earns one point.

Is a 4.0 UC GPA good?

It clears the 3.0 minimum for California residents and the 3.4 for nonresidents. Admission is competitive at many campuses, so treat the minimum as the starting line, not the target.
