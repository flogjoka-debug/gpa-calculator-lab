How to use the law school GPA calculator

  1. Pick your application cycle, because the dual enrollment rule changes with the 2027–2028 cycle.
  2. Add every graded undergraduate class from every school you attended, with the letter grade, credits and units. Include both attempts of any class you repeated, and mark classes you took while still in high school.
  3. Press Calculate to get your estimated LSAC GPA, with each class that counted and the reason for anything left out.

How LSAC calculates your GPA

LSAC converts every graded undergraduate class to its own 4.33 scale: A+ is 4.33, A is 4.00 and A− is 3.67. It counts every attempt at a repeated class that shows a grade and credits, even if your college replaced the old grade. Quarter hours are converted to semester hours by multiplying by 0.67. Work after your first bachelor's degree is left out.

Dual enrollment and your application cycle

In the 2026–2027 cycle, grades from college courses you took while in high school are part of your LSAC GPA. Starting with the 2027–2028 cycle, LSAC leaves them out for every applicant, including people who applied before. Law schools still see those transcripts in your CAS report.

Example: one class from high school

An A in a 3-credit undergraduate class and a B in a 3-credit class taken through dual enrollment.

2026–2027 cycle: (4.00 × 3 + 3.00 × 3) ÷ 6 = 21 ÷ 6 = 3.50.

2027–2028 cycle: the B is left out, so 12 ÷ 3 = 4.00.

Why your LSAC GPA can differ from your college GPA

Three rules cause most of the gap. An A+ is worth 4.33 instead of 4.0. Every attempt at a repeated class counts, even when your college replaced the first grade. And LSAC combines grades from every undergraduate school you attended, including community college, while your college GPA usually covers only its own classes.

What LSAC leaves out

Withdrawals and incompletes your school doesn't treat as failing, passing grades in pass/fail classes, remedial classes, AP test credit, and anything after your first bachelor's degree. A failing grade counts as 0.00, and so does a withdrawal your school treats as failing.

Already in law school?

Your law school grades don't count toward the LSAC GPA. Law schools set their own grading scales, often with a curve, so this calculator won't match your JD GPA. Your school's registrar or student handbook explains how it's calculated.

What changes for the 2027–2028 cycle

On August 18, 2026, LSAC announced that college courses taken while you were still in high school will leave the LSAC GPA starting with the 2027–2028 application cycle. Until then, in the 2026–2027 cycle, those grades are included. The change applies to everyone who applies in 2027–2028 or later, including people who applied before. Law schools still see those transcripts in your CAS report. Source: LSAC: Providing more time to adjust to LSAC GPA changes. Pick your cycle above, and you'll see which classes count.

How to check your transcripts before you apply

  • Request a transcript from every school you attended, including community colleges and summer programs that awarded college credit.
  • Find every repeated class. Each graded attempt counts, so include both here.
  • Look for withdrawals. A W your school treats as nonpunitive is left out, but one it treats as failing counts as 0.00.
  • Note which classes you took while still in high school, then pick your cycle above to see whether they count.

LSAC calculates the official number after you register for the Credential Assembly Service, but this estimate tells you early what it's likely to be. Grades from schools outside the U.S. and Canada usually aren't part of the LSAC GPA; LSAC sends law schools a separate foreign credential evaluation instead. For your own planning, the GPA converter gives a rough 4.0 equivalent.

How to estimate your LSAC GPA step by step

  1. Gather a transcript from every school where you took undergraduate classes before your first bachelor's degree, including community colleges.
  2. Convert each grade with LSAC's chart: an A+ is 4.33, an A− is 3.67 and a B+ is 3.33, whatever your school uses.
  3. Turn quarter hours into semester hours by multiplying by 0.67.
  4. Keep every graded attempt of a repeated class, and any withdrawal your school treats as failing, at 0.00. Leave out pass/fail passes, nonpunitive withdrawals, AP credit and work after your first bachelor's degree.
  5. Multiply each grade by its semester hours, add everything up and divide by the total hours.

The calculator does all five steps once you've entered your classes.

Example: two schools, one LSAC GPA

A student took 30 credits at a community college with a 3.8 GPA, then 90 credits at a university with a 3.4 GPA. The university's transcript shows a 3.40, because it counts only its own classes. LSAC combines both schools:

Frequently asked questions

Does LSAC count community college grades?

Yes. LSAC includes undergraduate work from every school you attended before your first bachelor's degree, each with its own transcript.

Does a W affect my LSAC GPA?

Not if your school treats it as nonpunitive. A withdrawal your school treats as failing counts as 0.00.

Do graduate school grades count in the LSAC GPA?

No. Anything after your first bachelor's degree is left out, although law schools still receive those transcripts.

How does LSAC convert quarter hours?

It multiplies quarter hours by 0.67 to get semester hours. A 4-unit quarter class counts as 2.68 semester hours.

Is this my official LSAC GPA?

No. LSAC calculates the official number from your transcripts after you register for the Credential Assembly Service. Use this estimate to check your math before you apply.

Does an A+ help my LSAC GPA?

Yes. LSAC counts an A+ as 4.33, even if your college caps it at 4.0, so each A+ raises the LSAC GPA a little above your college GPA.

Do retaken classes count twice for LSAC?

Each graded attempt counts once, so a class you took twice adds two grades. LSAC counts the earlier grade even when your college replaced it.

Do AP credits count in the LSAC GPA?

No. AP test credit has no grade, so LSAC leaves it out.

Does LSAC use my school's plus and minus values?

LSAC applies its own chart to every school instead, so an A− is 3.67 and a B+ is 3.33 even if your college uses 3.7 and 3.3.

Do community college grades raise or lower my LSAC GPA?

Either, depending on the grades. LSAC averages every undergraduate school by credits, so strong community college grades can lift your LSAC GPA above your university GPA.
