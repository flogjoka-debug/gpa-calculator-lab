What grade do I need on my final?

Take the grade you want, subtract the part of it your current grade already covers, and divide by the final's weight: score needed = (grade you want − current grade × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight. With an 85% now, a final worth 30% and a 90% goal, you'd need (90 − 85 × 0.7) ÷ 0.3 = 101.7%, so an A is out of reach without extra credit. A B (80%) needs 68.3%.

How to use the final grade calculator

  1. Enter your current grade in the class as a percentage, before the final.
  2. Enter the grade you want, or tap A, B, C or D, and the final exam's weight from your syllabus.
  3. Click Calculate. You'll see the score you need on the final, and whether it's possible.

How we calculated this

"Current grade" means your grade before the final. If the final is worth w of your grade, everything else is worth 1 − w. The score you need is (target − current × (1 − w)) ÷ w. We round it up to one decimal, so the number we show is always enough.

How to calculate what you need on your final

  1. Find your current grade, meaning your grade before the final.
  2. Find the final exam's weight in your syllabus and write it as a decimal (40% is 0.40).
  3. Multiply your current grade by 1 minus the weight. That's what the rest of the class contributes.
  4. Subtract that from the grade you want.
  5. Divide by the final's weight. The result is the score you need on the final.

Example: 85% now, aiming for 90%, final worth 40%

(90 − 85 × 0.60) ÷ 0.40 = (90 − 51) ÷ 0.40 = 97.5.

You need 97.5% on the final. With a perfect 100% you'd finish with 91%.

What if I need more than 100%?

Then that target is out of reach, unless your teacher offers extra credit. Say you have 80%, want 90%, and the final is worth 20%. You'd need 130%. Even a perfect final only brings you to 80 × 0.80 + 20 = 84%. We show that highest possible grade, so you can pick a target you can still reach.

Is my current grade really "before the final"?

Most online gradebooks show your grade with the work that's been graded so far, which usually doesn't include the final. If your final is in the same category as your tests, your teacher may weight it differently. Check the syllabus before you rely on the number.

How the final's weight changes the score you need

Starting from 85% and aiming for 90%, a heavier final gives you more room:

If the final is worth little, the grade you have now decides almost everything. That's why the calculator also shows the highest grade you can still reach. Not sure what your current grade is? Rebuild it from your scores with the grade calculator.

When the final is part of a category

Some syllabi don't give the final its own weight. Instead it sits in the test category with your other tests. Say tests are 60% of your grade, you've taken three tests worth 100 points each and scored 255, and the final is worth 200 points in the same category. Your other categories average 90 and make up the remaining 40%.

To finish with an 85, the test category needs (85 − 0.40 × 90) ÷ 0.60 = 81.7%. Across 500 test points that's about 408.3 points, so you need 154 out of 200 on the final, or 77%.

In this setup the final is really worth 60% × 200 ÷ 500 = 24% of your grade, not the 60% the test category carries.

When the final replaces your lowest test

Some teachers swap your lowest test score for your final exam score if the final is higher. The final then counts twice: once as the final and once in place of the weak test. Work it out in two steps. First find the score you need with the normal formula. Then check what that score does when it also replaces your lowest test. With a 60 on one test, any final above 60 raises your test average too, so the score you need is lower than the calculator shows. Ask your teacher exactly how the swap works before you plan around it.

If I fail the final, what will my grade be?

Your final grade is your current grade times the part of the course it covers, plus the final score times its weight: current × (1 − weight) + final × weight. With an 85% now and a final worth 30%:

Score on the finalFinal grade
0%59.5%

70%80.5%
100%89.5%

A zero on a 30% final drops an 85 below 60. Even a weak score keeps a lot more of your grade than skipping the exam.

How much does the final exam affect your grade?

The heavier the final, the more one exam moves your grade. Here's what a 60% on the final does to a class where you have an 85% now:

At 10%, a bad final costs you a few points. At 40%, it can cost you a whole letter grade.

What if my class uses points instead of percentages?

Then work in points. Multiply the percentage you want by the total points in the course, and subtract the points you already have. With 425 of 500 points so far and a 200-point final, a 90% needs 630 of 700 points: 205 on the final, which isn't possible. An 80% needs 560 points, so 135 of 200, or 67.5%. Converting to percentages first and entering them above gives the same answer, as long as the final's weight is its share of the total points, here 200 ÷ 700 = 28.6%.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need on my final to pass?

Set the grade you want to your class's passing cutoff, often 60% or 70%. With an 85% current grade and a final worth 40%, passing at 60% takes a 22.5% on the final.

What happens if I get a 0 on my final?

Your course grade becomes your current grade times 1 minus the final's weight. With 85% and a final worth 40%, that's 85 × 0.60 = 51%.

How do I find my final exam's weight?

Your syllabus usually lists it. If the final counts as one of your tests instead of its own category, use the Grade Calculator to test a few possible final scores instead.

Why is my teacher's number different from this result?

Teachers sometimes drop the lowest score, round, or weight the final differently. Use the exact weight and current grade from your gradebook, then check the syllabus for rounding rules.

How do I calculate my grade after the final?

Multiply your current grade by the part of the course it covers, then add your final score times the final's weight. With an 85% before a final worth 30% and a 78% on it: 85 × 0.7 + 78 × 0.3 = 82.9%.

Is 20% of your grade a lot for a final?

It's common, and it's enough to move you up to about a letter grade. With an 85% now, scores from 35% to 85% on a 20% final leave you anywhere from 75% to 85%.

If I have a 95 and fail the final, what will my grade be?

It depends on the final's weight. With a 95 and a final worth 20%, a 50% on the final gives 95 × 0.8 + 50 × 0.2 = 86%, and a zero gives 76%.

Can I get an A if I need more than 100% on the final?

Not on the exam alone. Ask whether there's extra credit, a curve or a way to replace a low score, and set a realistic target with the calculator.
