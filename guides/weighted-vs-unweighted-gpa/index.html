Weighted vs Unweighted GPA

By FloPixels · Updated November 23, 2026

An unweighted GPA counts every class the same way: an A is 4.0 whether it's in AP Chemistry or in study hall. A weighted GPA adds bonus points for harder classes such as Honors, AP and IB, so it can go above 4.0. Most high schools report one or both, and colleges read them differently.

What is an unweighted GPA?

It's the plain average of your grade points: A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1 and F = 0, with plus and minus grades in between if your school uses them. Class level doesn't matter.

How to calculate an unweighted GPA:

  1. Turn each final grade into its points on the 4.0 scale.
  2. If your classes carry different credits, multiply each grade's points by its credits.
  3. Add the points and divide by the number of classes, or by the total credits.

Example: four one-credit classes with grades A, A, B and C give (4 + 4 + 3 + 2) ÷ 4 = 13 ÷ 4 = 3.25.

What is a weighted GPA?

A weighted GPA starts from the same points and adds a bonus for advanced classes. A common setup is +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP and IB, which puts an A in an AP class at 5.0. Your school's handbook decides the exact bonus, which classes get it, and whether a D or an F still earns it.

How to calculate a weighted GPA:

  1. Find each class's base points on the 4.0 scale.
  2. Add your school's bonus to every Honors, AP or IB class.
  3. Average the results the same way, by class or by credits.

Example: the same four grades, where both A's are in AP classes and the B is in an Honors class. With +1.0 for AP and +0.5 for Honors you get (5 + 5 + 3.5 + 2) ÷ 4 = 15.5 ÷ 4 = 3.875, which is a 3.88 weighted GPA next to a 3.25 unweighted one.

To see both numbers for your own classes, use the high school GPA calculator. You can set your school's bonus points there.

Weighted GPA when classes have different credits

Many high schools give a yearlong class 1 credit and a semester class 0.5. Then you multiply each class's weighted points by its credits before you divide. Example: an A in AP Chemistry (1 credit, 5.0), a B in Honors English (1 credit, 3.5) and an A in a semester of Health (0.5 credit, 4.0). The weighted quality points are 5.0 + 3.5 + 2.0 = 10.5 over 2.5 credits, a weighted GPA of 4.20. Without the bonuses it's (4 + 3 + 2) ÷ 2.5 = 3.60. If you still need the semester grade itself, the semester grade calculator combines your quarters and the exam.

Which one do colleges use?

Colleges see the GPA your school puts on your transcript, and admissions readers also look at which courses you took. Because every high school weights classes its own way, some colleges recalculate GPAs with one set of rules for everyone. The University of California, for example, uses only A-G courses from 10th and 11th grade and caps honors points at 8. The UC GPA calculator shows how that works.

Is weighted or unweighted GPA better?

Neither is better. They answer different questions. The unweighted number shows how well you did in your classes. The weighted number also reflects how hard those classes were. When a form or application asks for one of them, give exactly that one.

How much does one AP class raise a weighted GPA?

Each bonus is spread across all your classes. With six one-credit classes in a year, one AP class at +1.0 lifts that year's weighted GPA by about 0.17 (1 ÷ 6), and three AP classes lift it by 0.50. Across four years and 24 classes, a single AP class adds about 0.04 to your cumulative weighted GPA. The bonus matters most in the years when you take several advanced classes at once.

Can a weighted GPA be lower than an unweighted GPA?

Not when your school only adds bonus points, because every class keeps at least its base value. If your weighted number is lower, the two GPAs probably don't include the same classes. Ask your counselor which courses each one counts before you compare them.

How to convert a weighted GPA to unweighted

Take out the bonus points and average the base grades again. An A in AP goes back to 4.0 and a B in Honors to 3.0. You can't do it from the final weighted number alone, because you need to know which classes earned a bonus.

Which GPA goes on the Common App?

The Common App asks for one cumulative GPA, its scale and whether it's weighted. Its guidance for schools says that when a school calculates both a weighted and an unweighted GPA, students should report the weighted one. The scale menu runs from 4 to 20, plus 100, so you can enter your school's real scale instead of converting. Source: Common App: High school details information sheet.

Colleges often recalculate it anyway

What you type isn't always what an admissions office uses. The University of Michigan tells applicants they can report either their cumulative or academic weighted GPA, and that it recalculates the GPA and uses its own number in review. Source: University of Michigan Admissions: Self-reporting your GPA. UC does the same with its own A-G rules, and Florida's Bright Futures scholarship recalculates a weighted GPA with a 0.5 bonus instead of a full point.

That's why you shouldn't spend energy comparing weighted GPAs from different schools. Look at the unweighted number and the courses behind it, which is roughly what recalculation does.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 4.0 weighted GPA good?

It can be, but it isn't the same as an unweighted 4.0. With bonus points, a weighted 4.0 can include B's. Check your unweighted GPA too before you compare it with a college's requirements.

Can an unweighted GPA be higher than 4.0?

On the standard scale, no. A few schools count an A+ as more than 4.0, and LSAC counts an A+ as 4.33 for law school applicants. You can see how in the law school GPA calculator.

What does a 5.0 GPA mean?

It usually means straight A's in classes that all earned a full one-point bonus, on a weighted 5.0 scale. Schools that weight differently can top out higher or lower.

Do colleges prefer a weighted or an unweighted GPA?

Neither by default. Admissions readers look at the GPA your school reports and at the courses behind it, and some colleges, such as the University of California and the University of Michigan, recalculate a GPA with their own rules.

How do I know if my GPA is weighted?

Check your transcript or report card. A GPA above 4.0, or one labeled weighted, includes bonus points. If it isn't labeled, your school's course catalog or your counselor can tell you.
