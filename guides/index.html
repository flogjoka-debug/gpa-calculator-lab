Start here

A GPA can mean three different numbers for the same student: the one your school prints, the one a college recalculates, and the one a scholarship or LSAC uses. These guides explain where the numbers come from, which rules change them, and what you can still do about yours. Each one cites the official source for every rule, such as UC Admissions, LSAC, the Common App or a university registrar, and shows the arithmetic step by step so you can check it with your own grades.

For high school students

Weighted vs unweighted GPA explains bonus points for Honors, AP and IB, and which number the Common App asks for. What is a good GPA? puts 2.5, 3.0, 3.5 and 4.0 in context, from the national average to scholarship cutoffs.

For college students

How to raise your GPA shows how much one semester can move a cumulative GPA, when a retake helps, and what a W does. The 4.0 GPA scale lists every letter grade with its points and percentage range, and shows how to convert a GPA from another scale.

For applicants

College, graduate school and law school applications all use GPA differently. Start with the guide on what counts as a good GPA, then use the UC GPA calculator or the law school GPA calculator for the exact rules those applications follow.

How we write these guides

Every guide is written and maintained by FloPixels, the studio that builds GPA Calculator Lab. We take rules from the organizations that set them, link the source next to the rule, and date each check. When a policy changes, such as LSAC's dual enrollment rule, we update the guide and the matching calculator together. Found something out of date? Tell us. Read more about GPA Calculator Lab.
