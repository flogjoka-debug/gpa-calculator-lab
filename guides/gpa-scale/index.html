The 4.0 GPA Scale

By FloPixels · Updated November 23, 2026

On the 4.0 scale most U.S. high schools and colleges use, an A is worth 4.0, a B 3.0, a C 2.0, a D 1.0 and an F 0. A plus or minus moves a grade by about 0.3. Your GPA is the average of those points, weighted by credits.

The percentages are a common example, not a national rule. Many schools use 90/80/70/60 cutoffs instead, and your syllabus has the final say. An A+ is usually worth 4.0, though some colleges give it 4.33.

How the scale turns grades into a GPA

Each grade's points are multiplied by the class's credits to give quality points. Add up the quality points and divide by the total credits. An A in a 3-credit class (12 points) and a B in a 4-credit class (12 points) give 24 ÷ 7 = 3.43. The GPA calculator does this for you and shows which classes counted.

How plus and minus grades change your GPA

On the standard 4.0 scale, a plus adds 0.3 and a minus takes 0.3 away: a B+ is 3.3, a B is 3.0 and a B− is 2.7. Some colleges use 3.67 and 3.33 instead of 3.7 and 3.3. Some high schools don't record plus or minus grades at all, so every B counts as 3.0.

That choice can move your GPA. Take four equal-credit classes with B+, B+, B+ and A−. With plus and minus grades the GPA is (3.3 + 3.3 + 3.3 + 3.7) ÷ 4 = 3.40. At a school that records only letters, the same work shows up as B, B, B and A, and the GPA is (3 + 3 + 3 + 4) ÷ 4 = 3.25. Plus and minus grades reward scores near the top of a letter and cost you points near the bottom.

How to convert a percentage to GPA

Don't put the percentage straight onto the 4.0 scale. Convert it to a letter grade first, using your school's cutoffs, then look up the points. If you only have scores, the grade calculator turns them into each class's percentage first.

  1. Find the letter grade for each class's final percentage. 88% is a B+ on the chart above.
  2. Look up that letter's grade points. A B+ is 3.3.
  3. Average the points across your classes, weighted by credits.

Averaging percentages first can give a different answer. Two equal-credit classes at 95% and 81% average 88%, which looks like a B+ and a 3.3. Converted one by one they are an A (4.0) and a B− (2.7), which average 3.35. Use the percentage to letter grade calculator to check each class against your own cutoffs.

What is a 3.5 GPA as a percentage?

There's no official conversion, because a GPA averages letter grades rather than percentages. A 3.5 sits between a B+ (3.3) and an A− (3.7). On the chart above that is roughly 89 to 90 percent, but on a 90/80/70/60 scale it would land elsewhere.

What is a 4.0 GPA?

A 4.0 is the highest unweighted GPA: an A in every class. On a weighted scale, Honors, AP and IB classes earn bonus points, so a weighted GPA can go above 4.0. Weighted vs unweighted GPA explains how.

Other scales you might see

  • Weighted 5.0 scale: high schools add up to one point for AP and IB classes.
  • 4.33 scale: some colleges give an A+ 4.33. LSAC uses it for every law school applicant, which you can try in the law school GPA calculator.
  • 100-point scale: some schools report averages as percentages and never convert them.

Grades that don't count on the scale

Some marks on a transcript carry no grade points and stay out of the average. A pass (P or S) earns credit but usually isn't part of the GPA. A W, for a class you dropped before the withdrawal deadline, is usually left out too. Other marks can count against you: at many colleges a WF is averaged in as an F. An incomplete usually waits until the final grade is posted. Your registrar's rules decide each one, and how to raise your GPA compares two real W and WF policies.

Turning a GPA back into a letter grade

Because a GPA is an average, it rarely lands exactly on a letter. Read it against the nearest grade points instead: a 3.2 sits between a B (3.0) and a B+ (3.3), closer to the B+. Schools don't assign a letter to your overall GPA, so on forms and resumes give the number and its scale, such as 3.2 out of 4.0.

Why your school's chart might differ

College Board's BigFuture uses the same pattern as the chart above: 93 to 96 is an A worth 4.0, and 90 to 92 is an A− worth 3.7. It also notes that the way letter grades convert to percentages can vary slightly between colleges, and even between departments in the same college. Source: College Board BigFuture: How to convert your GPA to a 4.0 scale. Treat any chart, including ours, as a guide and your syllabus as the rule.

How to convert a GPA from another scale

If your school reports GPA on a 5.0, 6.0 or 10-point scale, the quick conversion is your GPA × 4 ÷ your school's scale. A 4.1 on a 4.5 scale becomes 4.1 × 4 ÷ 4.5 = 3.64. A 7.16 on a 10-point scale becomes 2.86. Universities use this formula as an estimate, not an official conversion. The GPA converter does the math for any scale. Sources: UT Arlington: GPA converter, University of Arizona Graduate College: GPA calculator.

For a weighted high school GPA, this shortcut doesn't give you an unweighted GPA. Remove the bonus points class by class instead.

Frequently asked questions

What GPA is a B average?

A 3.0. A B+ average is a 3.3 and a B− average is a 2.7.

Is an A+ worth more than an A?

Usually not. Most schools count an A+ as 4.0, the same as an A. Some colleges and LSAC count it as 4.33.

What is a 3.0 GPA in percentage?

A 3.0 is a B, which is 83 to 86 percent on the chart above and 80 to 89 percent on a 90/80/70/60 scale.

Is the 4.0 scale the same in high school and college?

The letter values are usually the same, from A = 4.0 down to F = 0. The differences are weighting, which many high schools use and colleges usually don't, and whether plus and minus grades are recorded.

Do pass/fail classes count in your GPA?

Usually not. A pass earns credit without grade points, so it stays out of the average. Some schools do count a failing grade in a pass/fail class as an F, so check your registrar's policy.
