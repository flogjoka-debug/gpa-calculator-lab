What is a college GPA?

Your college GPA is the average of your grade points, weighted by each class's credit hours. Most U.S. colleges use a 4.0 scale with plus and minus grades, and unlike many high schools they don't add bonus points for honors classes. Your transcript shows a GPA for each term and a cumulative GPA for everything you've taken at that college.

How to use the college GPA calculator

  1. Enter each class's letter grade and credit hours. The class name is optional; it only labels the row.
  2. If you retook a class, add both attempts and mark the later one with "Retake of". The result then compares counting both attempts with replacing the earlier grade.
  3. Click Calculate GPA. You'll get your semester GPA, the quality points behind it and the credits that counted.

How we calculated this

Each grade has a point value on your school's scale. We multiply it by the class's credit hours to get quality points, add up the quality points, and divide by the credit hours that count toward GPA. A 4-credit class moves your GPA more than a 1-credit lab with the same grade.

How to calculate your semester GPA

Your semester GPA is the quality points you earned this term divided by the credit hours that count toward GPA.

  1. Find each class's credit hours on your schedule or transcript.
  2. Convert each letter grade to grade points using your college's scale, including plus and minus grades.
  3. Multiply grade points by credit hours for each class.
  4. Add the results and divide by the total GPA credit hours.

Example: an A in a 3-credit English class (4.0 × 3 = 12), a B+ in 4-credit Calculus (3.3 × 4 = 13.2), an A− in a 1-credit lab (3.7 × 1 = 3.7) and a C in 3-credit History (2.0 × 3 = 6). That's 34.9 quality points over 11 credits, so the semester GPA is 34.9 ÷ 11 = 3.17.

Example: retaking a class

You earned a D in a 3-credit class, then retook it and earned an A. The D is 1.0 × 3 = 3 quality points. The A is 4.0 × 3 = 12.

If your college counts both attempts: 15 ÷ 6 = 2.50.

If your college replaces the earlier attempt: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.00.

Does retaking a class replace the old grade?

It depends on your college. Some replace the earlier grade in your GPA, some count every attempt, and many limit how many classes you can repeat. The earlier grade usually stays on your transcript either way. Read your registrar's repeat policy, then choose the option that matches it.

One more thing if you plan to apply to law school: LSAC counts every attempt that appears on your transcript with a grade and credits, whatever your college does.

Semester GPA and cumulative GPA

A semester GPA covers one term. A cumulative GPA covers every graded term, weighted by credits. Add more semesters here to see both, or bring in a total from your transcript with the cumulative calculator.

What counts toward a college GPA

An F counts as zero points, and its credits stay in the total.

P, NP and W are left out here because they usually carry no grade points. Some colleges count a WF as an F, so check before you trust the number.

An A+ is worth 4.0 at many colleges and more at others. Edit the scale if yours differs.

On the quarter system, enter quarter units as your credits. The math is the same.

Retake rules at real colleges

Grade replacement is common, but every college puts limits on it. Here's how three registrars describe theirs:

  • UCLA lets undergraduates repeat a class to raise their GPA only after a C− or lower. For the first 16 repeated units only the newest grade counts; after that, every grade is averaged in. Source: UCLA Registrar: Repetition of Courses.
  • UC Berkeley allows a repeat after a D+, D, D−, F or NP. Both grades stay on the transcript, but only the most recent one counts, for the first 12 graded units of repeats. Source: Berkeley Office of the Registrar: Grading policies.
  • The University of Cincinnati asks students to request a grade replacement and caps it at 18 semester credit hours. Source: University of Cincinnati: Grade Replacements.

Your college's catalog has its own version. Look up the grade you need before a retake counts, and how many repeats are allowed, then pick "Replace the earlier grade" or "Count both attempts" above to match. Policies checked November 22, 2026.

How to calculate your major GPA

Many programs look at a major GPA, built only from the classes that count toward the major, before you can declare it or graduate. The math doesn't change: list only those classes, multiply each one's grade points by its credits and divide by the credits. Your degree audit usually shows which classes your college counts, and some include required classes from other departments. Enter just those classes above to get the number.

What is a good college GPA?

It depends on what the GPA is for. Federal financial aid rules require students in programs longer than two years to have at least a C average, or its equivalent, by the end of their second academic year (34 CFR 668.34). Majors, honors programs, scholarships and graduate schools set their own minimums, often higher. Our guide to what counts as a good GPA has examples with sources. Coming from a school that grades on a 10-point, 20-point or percentage scale? The GPA converter shows roughly where your GPA lands on the 4.0 scale.

How to raise your college GPA

  • Put your effort where the credits are. Turning a B into an A in a 4-credit class adds 4 quality points, twice as much as the same change in a 2-credit class.
  • Retake a low grade if your college replaces it, within the limits described above.
  • Know your withdrawal deadline. A W usually stays out of the GPA, while an F counts as zero.
  • Work backward from a target with the target GPA planner on our main calculator.

Each new term moves the GPA less than the one before, which our guide on how to raise your GPA shows with numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How many credits is a typical college class?

Many lecture classes carry 3 or 4 credit hours, and labs often carry 1. Your course schedule or transcript lists the exact number for each class.

Does a W hurt my GPA?

A W usually carries no grade points and isn't counted in your GPA. It still stays on your transcript, and withdrawals can matter for financial aid progress rules, so talk to your advisor before you drop.

Is an A+ worth more than 4.0 in college?

At many colleges an A+ is capped at 4.0. Some give it 4.33. Set the A+ value in the scale above so it matches your transcript.

Do transfer credits count in my college GPA?

At many colleges, transfer credits count toward your degree but not toward the GPA at your new school. Your registrar's GPA policy says how yours handles them.

How do I calculate GPA on the quarter system?

The same way. Enter quarter units as your credits. If you need semester hours, for example for a law school application, multiply quarter units by 0.67.

Is college GPA weighted?

Usually not. Colleges grade every class on the same scale and weight each grade by credit hours, not by difficulty. An A in an honors seminar and an A in an intro class are both 4.0.

Do pass/fail classes count in my college GPA?

A passing grade usually adds credits to your transcript but no grade points, so it doesn't change your GPA. Some colleges count a failing grade in a pass/fail class as an F, so check your catalog.

What if a grade isn't posted yet?

Enter the grade you expect to get an estimate, or leave that row blank and calculate without it. Blank rows stay out of the math until you fill them in.

Can I round my GPA up on an application?

Report it the way your transcript shows it unless the application says how to round. Turning a 3.47 into a 3.5 can read as a misstatement when the school checks your transcript.
